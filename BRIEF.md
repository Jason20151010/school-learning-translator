# Project brief: School learning translator

## The competition
- Presidential SI Challenge (Standard Track), national K–12 competition.
- Age group: Elementary (grades K–5).
- Judges score a written entry (PDF up to 10 pages) and a video up to 4 minutes.
- Deadlines: registration December 18 at 5:00 PM ET; project submission December 23, 2026 at 5:00 PM ET / 2:00 PM PT.

## The problem
- People in school struggle with language because of school, family, or other situations.
- Impact area: School and learning.
- Students and families need to understand school emails, activities, and learning words.
- The American Speech-Language-Hearing Association reports that developmental language disorder affects about 7.5% of children.
- Yunze Du said his mom cannot understand what school emails mean and wants a good translator.

## My solution
- Help students learn language more easily, not just translate it.
- Explain words, give examples, translate into a home language, and make practice quizzes.
- Google Translate translates; this app translates and helps students learn.
- Safety plan: use a safe, limited experience for children age seven and older, with simple answers and privacy protections.

## My MVP features (build these first, in this order)
1. The user can type a word and get an AI explanation and example sentence.
2. The user can translate a word or sentence into their home language.
3. The user can take a short quiz on the words they looked up.
4. The AI gives correct, safe answers to real questions.

## My AI’s instructions
- The user types what they want and presses Enter or clicks Check.
- Explain and translate in an easy-to-understand way and make a practice test for students.
- Talk to students and families in friendly, simple language.
- Keep answers short, clear, and age appropriate.
- Protect children’s privacy. Do not share names, messages, voice, location, or school information without permission. Do not save private student text for training without clear consent. Do not make up answers when unsure. Do not help identify, contact, track, or exploit a child. Do not make decisions about grades, discipline, health, or safety. Do not pretend to be a teacher, parent, counselor, or emergency service. Do not use targeted ads or hidden tracking. Do not bypass parent or school privacy rules.
